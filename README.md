# 🧮 Quantitative Methods I — Problem Sets  
**Master in Competition, Regulation & Markets (BSE CRM 2025)**  

Applied econometrics exercises from the *Quantitative Methods I* course of the Master in *Competition, Regulation & Markets* at the **Barcelona School of Economics (BSE)**. Includes analytical derivations and empirical applications using **Stata** and **Python**.

---

## 📘 Overview  

This repository compiles the complete set of **Problem Sets (PS1–PS5)** for *Quantitative Methods I (QSM)*, a core course in the CRM program at BSE.  
The course provides a rigorous foundation in **econometric estimation, inference, and model specification**, bridging theoretical principles with empirical research practice.

Each assignment combines analytical derivations with practical applications, aiming to build fluency in interpreting econometric results, understanding estimation properties, and evaluating model assumptions.

---

## 🧩 Problem Sets  

### **PS1 — Linear Regression Analysis**  
Focuses on the classical **Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)** framework and the conditions under which estimators are unbiased and efficient.  
Students derive key results (Gauss–Markov theorem, hypothesis testing) and interpret the implications of violating core assumptions such as homoskedasticity or exogeneity.  
Includes both analytical derivations and simulated examples using Stata to visualize estimator behavior.  

---

### **PS2 — Elasticities and Nonlinear Models**  
Explores **functional transformations** of regression models, with emphasis on **log-linear and semi-log specifications** and the interpretation of coefficients as elasticities.  
Exercises include fitting nonlinear models, computing marginal effects, and assessing model fit relative to linear alternatives.  
Students practice interpreting elasticity estimates in economic contexts such as demand analysis and productivity functions.  

---

### **PS3 — Structural Breaks and Instrumental Variables**  
Introduces methods to detect **structural instability** (Chow test, recursive estimation) and tackles **endogeneity** through **Instrumental Variable (IV)** estimation.  
Assignments guide the derivation of the IV estimator, its consistency properties, and implementation via Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS).  
Concepts of instrument validity and overidentification are empirically tested through examples involving policy changes and measurement error.  

---

### **PS4 — Generalized Method of Moments (GMM)**  
Develops the theory and application of **GMM estimation**, emphasizing the role of **moment conditions** and the weighting matrix in achieving efficiency.  
Exercises involve deriving optimal GMM estimators, performing overidentification tests, and contrasting GMM with OLS and IV approaches.  
Numerical implementation in Stata and Python allows comparison of estimators’ small-sample behavior.  

---

### **PS5 — Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**  
Covers the principles of **maximum likelihood**, including likelihood construction, log-likelihood functions, and numerical optimization routines.  
Students derive and estimate parameters for nonlinear models (e.g., logit/probit, exponential) and study **asymptotic properties** such as consistency and normality of MLE estimators.  
The problem set concludes with Monte Carlo simulations illustrating convergence behavior and standard error estimation.  

---

## ⚙️ Technical Details  

Each problem set includes:
- **Analytical solutions** (derivations, proofs, and test statistics)
- **Empirical applications** in *Stata* and *Python*
- **Interpretative discussions** connecting econometric theory to policy and market analysis  

The material serves as a bridge to advanced courses such as *Quantitative Methods II*, *Applied Econometrics*, and *Empirical Industrial Organization*.
